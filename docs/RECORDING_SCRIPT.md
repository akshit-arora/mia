# Voice recording script (English)

Read this once, then record scene by scene. The goal is **warm and playful, not professional**. A child should feel a friendly person is smiling at them.

## 1. The voice in one line

Talk **to** a child, not **at** them: a little slower than normal, clear vowels, a smile you can hear, and big, friendly ups and downs in pitch. Use a neutral Indian-English accent, the kind children hear at school.

Do not sound like a teacher, a news reader or an announcer. Never sound disappointed.

## 2. Two characters, one voice

| Character | Sounds like | How to do it |
|---|---|---|
| **Bholu** (truck, the doer) | A cheerful, excitable older cousin | Your natural voice, a bit brighter. Short, bouncy lines. |
| **Chiku** (sparrow, the wonderer) | A curious friend whispering a secret | Softer, breathier, slightly higher and slower. Pitch rises on questions. If it still sounds too similar, we can lift it slightly in the build script. |
| **Names** (animal names, zone names) | A proud, happy reveal | Bholu's voice. Same energy for all of them. |

## 3. Setup (10 minutes)

- Use a phone voice memo, or any recorder app that saves WAV or M4A.
- Record in a small room with soft things around: a closet with clothes, or under a blanket.
- Hold the phone 15 to 20 cm from your mouth, slightly to the side, and **keep the same distance every time**.
- Switch the phone to do-not-disturb. Turn off fans and AC.
- Leave **half a second of silence** before and after each line.
- Record **3 takes per line**. We pick the best.
- Name each file with the **key** from the tables below (for example `correct.1.wav`, `name.lion.wav`).
- Plan **two sessions of about 30 minutes**. Drink water, rest your voice, and record the names all in one go so they match.

## 6. After recording

The build script will trim silence, match loudness, convert to a small mono file and build the audio pack. A coverage report will list any missing keys. Each line also appears as a **caption** with the same text, so what you say here is exactly what a deaf or hard-of-hearing child reads.
